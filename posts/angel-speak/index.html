Let SSS be the set of all meaningful sentences. For the sake of argument, let us agree that a meaningful sentence is a sentence that does not defy logic and that is possible.

Statement 1. SSS is infinite
The English sentence I have one apple implies that you can have sentences of the form I have xxx apple(s), where xxx is a natural number (x∈Nx \in \mathbb{N}x∈N). Let us denote the set of such sentences claiming the possession of a number of apples as

A={I have x apple(s)∣x∈N}A = \{\text{I have $x$ apple(s)} \mid x\in \mathbb{N} \} A={I have x apple(s)∣x∈N}

There is no upper bound on xxx, so ∣A∣=ℵ0|A|=\aleph_0∣A∣=ℵ0​, and since A⊂SA \subset SA⊂S, SSS is infinite. ■\blacksquare■

Statement 2. SSS is uncountable (i.e., ∣S∣≥2ℵ0|S|\geq 2^{\aleph_0}∣S∣≥2ℵ0​)
The English sentence I have a one-meter rod, since the length of a rod can be arbitrary, implies that you can have sentences of the form I have an xxx-meter-long rod, where xxx is a real number. Let us denote the set of such sentences claiming the possession of a rod of arbitrary length (any real number) as

L={I have an x-meter-long rod∣x∈R}L = \{\text{I have an $x$-meter-long rod} \mid x\in \mathbb{R}\} L={I have an x-meter-long rod∣x∈R}

Then ∣L∣=2ℵ0|L|=2^{\aleph_0}∣L∣=2ℵ0​, and since L⊂SL\subset SL⊂S, ∣S∣≥2ℵ0|S| \geq 2^{\aleph_0}∣S∣≥2ℵ0​. ■\blacksquare■

Examples of elements of LLL are sentences like:

I have a 42-meter rod
I have a 2\sqrt{2}2​-meter rod
I have a π\piπ-meter rod
I have a googol-meter rod
